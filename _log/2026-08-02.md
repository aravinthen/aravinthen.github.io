## Foundation models
1. Busy Sunday - didn't manage to implement anything (and plus, I appreciated a quieter day). 
2. I did however read through Chapters 6 and 7 from Raschka. Not much was here, really - this was quite intuitive given my previous interests in machine learning. It appears that fine-tuning is just training with very specific data formats. 
    * The initial process of training a model is self-supervised learning. The text generates an output and the calculates a loss from its generated token and the action token to be predicted. That output is unlabelled, or rather automatically labelled - it is generated automatically from the raw data provided to the model. 
    * Supervised learning comes after training - by using explicit pairs of features and labels (curated by humans), one can provide effective support expansion and alignment to widen and control the scope of trajectories that the foundation model is capable of. 
    *  Reinforcement learning is then used for policy reshaping, where the best behaviours that a model can generate are made more likely through critiquing the data that is actually generated by the model. 
3. Foundation models are the ultimate form of the machine learning techniques of the 2010s. Pretty much everything is used... absolutely marvellous.
