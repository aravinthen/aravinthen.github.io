## Summary
1. Managed to whip up a demo for a co-monitor project that I'm developing with a start-up. Stage one - done!

## AI Safety
1. I spent most of the day testing the demo of the hierarchical co-monitor set-up I started working on three days ago. 
2. Main result is that the models are *not* capable of reliably making changes without active post-training. Many cases of false alarms and a *measurable* failure in the pre-trained safety monitor.
3. I'm currently trying to use my Adaption Labs credits to generate data from this. This is proving difficult...
4. There hierarchical set-up is simple enough. I'm using a bunch of auto-generated stories to represent a story directive from a cloud model, with a small language model (open weights) to carry out story personalisation. I'm then using a smaller model as the co-monitor, which takes in the original story and the personalized story to deliver a safety assessment. 
5. Providing the personalized and original story is important: we're trying to gauge whether the personalized story is *similar enough* to the original. This is a pretty important design decision: we don't want the story to drift!
6. I am simultaneously beginning to design an adversarial self-play approach where the full model stack is trained to be fully robust to adversarial prompts from the original model. I think there's something quite powerful here, because the personalisation model should have the ability to convert a *broad* variety of dangerous inputs to something that is verifiable as child-friendly. 
7. There's a theory of change hidden here: instead of simply flagging whether your outputs are safe or unsafe, why not *consistently* change them to be safe no matter what? I'm not going to go too deep into this just yet, but it's worth thinking about.
