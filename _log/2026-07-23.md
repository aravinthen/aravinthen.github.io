## Summary
1. More reading of *Large Language Model Post-Training: A Unified View of Off-Policy and On-Policy Learning* by Zhao et al. 

## Literature review
1. Examples of off-policy fine-tuning include a variety of supervised training examples.
2. On-policy fine-tuning, on the other hand, is based around the standard reward signals that arise in the RL problem. All of the methods discussed are in some way related to *rewards*, even if "reward" is used here as a proxy for a convex additive value assigned to a given trajectory.
3. Do foundation models and their environments have *deletion* capabilities? Can a model be augmented with the ability to *remove* some of it's text to walk back on it's own answer and adjust it's probability for an external reward? Reading about the framing of post-training as  an occupancy problem makes me think along these lines.
4. Note that "visiting a prefix" means "choosing a word to append to the full prediction". Interesting, but I'm wondering how this has interplay with the Markov assumptions inherent to RL.
5. Aha. The prompt. That's an important piece of this problem and is represented as a conditional upon the autoregressor distribution. 
6. **Prefix reachability**: whether useful intermediate states are visited with non-negligible mass under the policy.
7. **State-conditional action availability**: whether useful actions are generated once those states are reached.
8. ...aren't the two closely related? I see the distinction, but I'm not sure why these are complementary.
9. One risk that I'm not seeing mentioned is the prevention of *damage* that might occur to the original foundational model during post-training.
