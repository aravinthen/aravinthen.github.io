## Summary
1. Finished *Large Language Model Post-Training: A Unified View of Off-Policy and On-Policy Learning* by Zhao et al. 

## Literature review
1. This was a very useful paper. It provided some really strong mental models and by the end of it, the future directions of post-training: that is, as a systems problem over just a few routines, were pretty much inferable from the premise. 
2. Most interesting were the aspects on the mechanistic interpretability that is inherent to these models, as well as the reasons that the different regimes of post-training (effective support expansion, policy reshaping and distillation) even occur. 
3. It seems relatively straightforward as to why on-policy and off-policy training results in policy reshaping/effective support, but when you think about it for more than six minutes you realise... why can't you do effective support expansion or policy reshaping with all methods? Why is it that *particular* mechanisms lead to particular results?
4. The measurement of these outputs and effects are also very poorly specified, it seems like.  Granted, the paper was written ~~some time ago~~ a few months ago. I wonder how much as changed since then?

## Remarks
1. Whew! What a paper. This was a nasty paper to read, though. Very, very wordy, although the content was really interesting.
2. This field of post-training is really, really damned interesting. I'm quite glad that I made this jump from standard reinforcement learning to LLM post-training, just due to the relevance of my research to the wider technical field. 
3. That being said, I wonder how much interplay LLM post-training has with advanced RL techniques like multi-agent RL and partial observability? Are there analogues that emerge when LLMs are trained instead?
