## Game theory
1. Discovered a new approach to the problem of studying games: *mechanism design*, or reverse game theory. This is apparently a very important methodology in the design of markets, something that is fast becoming an important feature in the swarm alignment project plan and my broader interests in alignment as a whole...
2. The idea here is to start with a fixed goal and work backward to build a system where participants naturally settle on truthful actions and cooperation. 
3. Interestingly, I'm realising that the actual practice of mechanism design is pretty much the same as optimisation. In fact, I think it might be *identical*. 
    * Define the objective that you want to achieve and identify a set of feasibile outcomes.
    * Model the participants and the information they have access to.
    * Formulate the necessary constraints. There's something interesting here in that there seem to be foundational constraints that are necessary: a participation constraint which basically makes it so that sitting out is sub-optimal, as well as information sharing rules (revelation mechanisms).
    * Set up the dynamics of the problem that allow the ideal strategies to result from the above to be discovered.
4. A simple example of mechanism design: two children deciding how much of a cake they get. The child who cuts the cake will naturally want to cut a bigger piece for herself, so what can one do?
5. Solution: *the first child cuts the cake, the second child chooses which piece they want*. 
6. There's a huge amount to this field, but I need to spend a good evening sitting down and reviewing materials before I form an opinion on this...
