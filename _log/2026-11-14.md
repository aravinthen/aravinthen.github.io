## Game theory
1. Refreshing my game theory knowledge in preparation for AI debate work.
2. 'Decision makers interacting'. That is the essence of game theory.
3. This is the first time that I've actually seen a working definition of what 'rationality' means. 
    * Rationality: where decision makers pursue well-defined exogenous objectives - this means that the objectives themselves exist *externally* to the system that carries out the decision-making.
    * Strategic: this is where decision-makers actively count their knowledge/expectations of the other decision-makers in the scenario. 
4. A lot of people crap on game theory for being too abstract. O&R argue that it is this abstraction that gives it the power to study a wide range of phenomena.
5. *Interesting*. A new concept! The *core* is an idea in game theory that describes the set of allocations that are possible to sustain cooperation between decision makers. 
6. Interestingly, the authors actively label their treatment of game theory as 'social science'.  
7. There are four groups of games that are described in this book: strategic, or normal games; extensive games with perfect information and without perfect information and coalitional games. I investigated examples of *different games* and found *evolutionary games*, *differential games* (the continuous time analogue), Markov games, behavioural games and mean-field games. 
8. The games can also be described in terms of cooperative and non-cooperative games. The theory of the core, for instance, related to cooperative games: mathematically these are distinguished by *sets of joint actions* for the cooperative cases versus *sets of possible actions* for the non-cooperative case. 
9. *There is a difference in philosophy to note*. The theory of competitive equilibrium as applied to economics describes a scenario where prices are set due to the actions of other agents, but are treated as independent environment signals. Unlike in game theory, there is no specific out-maneuvering of agents and adversaries.
