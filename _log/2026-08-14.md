## Summary
1. More work on Raschka's reasoning models book whilst on the train.

## Reasoning
1. The portrayal of reasoning that Raschka gives is magnificent. There is no pretense that any of this is how people actually think: the essence of it is that these are means by which LLMs can be made to mimic reasoning or, at the very least, take intermediate steps.
2. I found inference-time scaling to be the suprisingly interesting part of this whole process. **Chain of thought reasoning** was the interesting part here, where even fixed models are capable of generating some kind of reasoning steps. 
3. These techniques are beyond powerful. I wonder how general they are, though? I get the feeling that the basic ideas behind the foundation model were pretty generic: I can see, for instance, how a generic model might be able to represent a number of interesting circumstances. Can reasoning also be used in a variety of circumstances or are these ideas limited to language-based technology?
