## Literature review
1. The distinction between on-policy and off-policy post-training techniques is wonderfully useful. Of *course* SFT is off-policy! The distinction makes perfect sense!
2. The separation of off-policy as being a powerful means of effective support expansion whereas on-policy techniques tend to focus on policy reshaping seems natural in hindsight. Off-policy methods unlock desirable behaviour when done right, whereas on-policy results typically strengthen the model assuming that the desirable behaviour is reachable.]
3. The paper also very clearly motivates the reason that hybrid models are the inevitable result of the post-training workflow.
4. "SFT → RL → distillation. This pattern is increasingly important in reasoning- and agent-heavy systems". Sounds about right! I'm also intrigued with the possibility of the interleaving approach. 
5. I wonder what the limit is here? Is there an ultimate point where this kind of training falls apart? Feels like there ought to be an information-theoretic compression limit that emerges...
