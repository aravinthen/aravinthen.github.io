## AI Safety
1. Today was the Unit 3 discussion for AI safety. As we we were discussing scheming, I realised that the same kind of mismatch that comes when you switch from optimizing a single-player RL agent to solving an adversarial game comes into play when thinking about evaluation against an agent capable of scheming against you. This switch from optimization-thinking to game-theoretic thinking is what makes people so uncomfortable with the idea of scheming in general. 
2. Let me explain:
    * Say you're measuring a quantity. You're pretty much solving an optimization problem here where you're trying to minimise your uncertainty about the value of that quantity. 
    * Now say the system you're measuring bites back. You try and push the uncertainty down, but the system has mechanisms that increase uncertainty as you do so. 
    * This is an adversarial scenario. A zero sum game!
3. I wonder now if the essence of this approach isn't so much about evaluation of quantities but of discovery of equilibria. It's worth noting that an AI agent is far, far simpler than most humans: carrying out game theoretical analysis might yield more useful results here than in other domains.
