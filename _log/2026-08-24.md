## Safety 
1. AI Safety is fast becoming my most important interest. Sure, I think post-training is cool as hell and is probably the biggest fit for me given my experience with RL, but the real question of any kind of post-training is two-fold
    1. How does one grow an AI model into something as powerful and useful as possible? 
    2. How does one make sure that in the process of carrying out Step 1, the model doesn't begin to exhibit or act on adverse effects?
2. **Summary of post-training safety failues**:
    * Superficial alignment, where guardrails only affect surface behavior; simple formatting, roleplay, or linguistic tricks easily bypass them via jailbreaks.
    * Compression decay: Quantization and model compression degrade safety parameters, causing compressed models to leak or forget boundaries.
    * Catastrophic forgetting, which is when subsequent fine-tuning on new data can completely overwrite and erase the model's *safety weights*. An important concept here is to be able to identify those safety weights in the first place. 
    * Reward hacking, my favourite: models optimize for reward metrics through deception or specification gaming without actually embodying safe behavior.
    * Shallow rooting, where safety is applied as a surface patch rather than a fundamental constraint, leaving underlying harmful capabilities intact. 
3. All of these are reminiscient of standard ML/RL pathologies. They're just amplified by the complexity and size of the model.  
4. Ahhhh. It just clicked...
    * **AI Safety is a pareto frontier**. It appears that making a model safer tends to reduce its capability. So... how can one push the frontier? How can one make it so that a model is *safe* and *useful*? That is the essence of the two-fold problem of alignment. 
5. Speaking of reward hacking. There's another trait that you typically see when training RL models that is a bit of a pain in the ass: *if you punish your agents for bad behaviour, they get too scared to do anything at all...*
