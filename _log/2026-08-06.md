## Summary
1. Studied reinforcement learning with human feedback.

## Foundation models
1. My first foray into *reasoning*. Spent a quiet evening looking through how RLHF works and the basic techniques required to enable it.
2. It's funny how much of this depends on using LoRA. You have to take this massive, powerful model and the attach some tiny filters to modify the output of the model to suit your intended purpose. There's something very... interesting about this approach. I wonder why it's necessary in the first place?
3. RLHF isn't so difficult, really - the critical stage is the reward model, which replaces the hand-crafted reward inherent in RL with a learned representation that is obtained from preference data. This is very, very intuitive stuff. 
4. Something tells me that these techniques - RLHF and LoRA SFT - are very, very temporary. They just don't strike me as techniques that maximise the raw power that token prediction machines are capable of generating. I can already see it as the base model being the "linguistic imagination" of the system that constantly generates words, whereas the fine-tuning and reasoning side employ this imagination to generate thought. 
5. I must keep studying. These are absolutely marvellous machines. I also need to start reading neuroscience literature.
