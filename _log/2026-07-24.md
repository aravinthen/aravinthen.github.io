## Summary
1. More reading of *Large Language Model Post-Training: A Unified View of Off-Policy and On-Policy Learning* by Zhao et al. 

## Literature review
1. The complexity here for me is the distinction between effective support expansion and policy reshaping. 
2. So... effective support expansion unlocks new, hard-to-reach or previous unattainable reasoning paths. Policy reshaping makes it so that paths that the policy can *already* reach are found more reliably. They're distinguished by the probabilities associated with each prefix - effective support expansion broadens the probability distribution whereas policy reshaping sharpens the probability peaks of desired values. Got it.
3. Support attrition is a consequence - it tracks the forgetting of the model between post-training instances. Got it. 
4. **Behavioural consolidation**, however, is a systems component that manages how the behaviours you get from the distribution level changes are preserved and stabilized over multiple post-training stages. This is eye-opening: before I was thinking about post-training as something you just did after getting a foundation model. It's more likely that modern systems are stages of growth and shaping. This... is somewhat eerily biological. This is the stage of the process that is concerned with the damage I was talking about yesterday.
5. The stages here are
    * **Retention**: preserving the good changes,
    * **Transfer**: carrying throuhg the good changes,
    * **Amortization**: reducing expensive and convoluted supporting processes into simple and direct analogues (so essentially short-cutting).
    * **Compression**: reducing the good changes into a cheaper model.
6. Consolidation is the opposite of attrition.
