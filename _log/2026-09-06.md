## Software engineering
1. I keep seeing it now. The parallels to systems thinking, where the systems here are simplified and highly precise. Stocks and flows abound, be they into a stack or a heap or in a more abstract form, as accumulated values over a graph.
2. Algorithms are pretty much just engineered dynamical systems. The resources that are used when designing such a system are time and space - there's a natural means of organising these in the most efficient way possible.
3. Just to note, **systems thinking** as a discipline and problem solving approach is based around assessing how the individual parts of a system interact, influence each other and function as a whole over time. 
4. Take the problem of finding the K closest points to the origin in a list of points. You need a measure of state that preserves a useful invariant - the K closest points you've seen so far. The stock here is a list of numbers and your goal is to make it so that the inflow (the points you're seeing) maintains the state of "the K smallest points seen so far". 
5. That's as far as my systems knowledge goes. But let's do this properly. My software engineering practice is now going to be a deliberate means of studying systems thinking in safe, ideal conditions. I'm going to learn how to represent systems through states, boundaries, transitions, invariants and information flows.
