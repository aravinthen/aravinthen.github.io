## Reinforcement learning
1. And we're back to RL, this time with more focus on the HF side. :) 
2. The first chapter keeps talking about "other post-training tools", which aren't quite discussed because the book focuses entirely on the RLHF paradigm. What are those other tools?
    * SFT, which transforms the auto-complete into a text-based assistant. More specifically we're looking at *IFT*, the technique that specifically allows an LLM to answer and act on instructions. 
    * Context extension, which consists of techniques that allow the model to adapt to much longer context windows than in the original training process.
    * RLHF, which I'll be reading about anyway. There are also extensions such as RLVF and RLAIF. 
    * Efficiency tools like LoRA/QLoRA and knowledge distillation.
3. IFT is what transforms the model into an instruction follower. RLHF takes these answers and make them *useful*. 
4. It's interesting that Lambert notes that there's a difference between thoughtful style training and sheer benchmark optimisation. Aiming for the latter gives you a product, whereas optimizing for a benchmark gives you a superficial, brittle output.
5. *RL-based models generalize across domains*. That means that an RL trained model will likely be able to apply its skills to new, unseen tasks whereas IFT tends to make the good at only *some* tasks. That's a super useful distinction!
6. IFT teaches the model what to say, whereas RL teaches the model how to think. That is, how to explore and try different approaches to satisfy a given task.
7. The *Build a Large Language/Reasoning Model* books were really good preparation for this book.
8. "The best post-training teams extract a great deal of performance in a very short time", using techniques such as mid-training, end-of-pretraining web data, instruction fine-tuning, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, preference tuning, etc... and it is in the etc that I want more information from!
9. **The Elicitation Theory of Post Training**: base models determine the vast majority of the potential of a fina model. Post-training is meant to cultivate *all* of it. 
10. Likewise, the *superficial alignment hypothesis* is the idea that post-training simply teaches the right style or behavioural disposition as opposed to raw capability, the latter of which is presumed to be obtained during the training stage. This is widely thought to be a simplification.
11. A new idea: *scaled post-training*. The expansion of the compute capability assigned to the post-training stage.
12. **Post-training: the craft of elliciting powerful behaviours from a raw pretrained model**.
